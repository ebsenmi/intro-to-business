Section 2 of 5 — Sort and Classify

🎲 Planning Types

Four planning scenarios. Click the labeled button that matches the correct planning type for each one.

★ 4 points ● 1 attempt only
Read each planning scenario below. Click the button that matches the correct planning type. Each scenario has exactly one correct answer. You have one attempt — choose carefully.
Strategic Planning
Long-range (1–5 years), broad organizational goals set by top management.
Tactical Planning
Shorter time frame (under 1 year), specific objectives supporting strategic goals.
Operational Planning
Specific standards, methods, and procedures for daily functional activities.
Contingency Planning
Alternative courses of action for unusual or crisis situations.
After a major winter storm causes road closures, the VP of Logistics activates a documented emergency plan that outlines who takes command, which suppliers to contact for emergency restocking, and which stores to prioritize for delivery.
The produce department manager creates a daily stocking checklist, defines exactly how much of each item to order based on historical sales by day of week, and sets a standard that no item should be out of stock for more than 20 minutes.
A grocery chain CEO announces a five-year plan to expand into 12 new metropolitan markets, acquire a regional organic food brand, and become the top-rated grocer for fresh produce in the country.
The district sales manager designs an eight-month campaign targeting college-town locations, sets monthly revenue targets for each store, and allocates $40,000 in local advertising to support the chain's national expansion strategy.

Please assign each scenario to a planning type before submitting.

Section 3 of 5 — Matching Dropdowns

🤝 Leadership Styles

Read each manager description and select the leadership style it best represents using the dropdown menu.

★ 5 points ● 1 attempt only
Read each manager description and select the leadership style it best represents from the dropdown. You have one attempt — all five are scored when you click Submit.
Natasha manages a team of veteran architects. She gives them their project brief and budget, then steps back entirely. The team sets their own timelines, divides responsibilities however they see fit, and presents finished work at the end of the project. Natasha reviews only the final deliverable.
During a factory explosion, the plant manager immediately takes command. She tells every worker exactly where to go, assigns specific roles without asking for input, and makes all evacuation decisions herself until the crisis is resolved.
Before deciding whether to launch a new product line, Jerome asks every team member to share their perspective in a meeting. After the discussion, he asks the group to vote. The option with the most votes becomes the team's official direction.
Before making any major operational decision, Lena meets individually with each of her department heads to understand their perspective and gather information. After these conversations, Lena makes the final call herself, incorporating what she has learned.
When her department needs to choose between two software platforms, Maya brings the full team together, facilitates a detailed discussion, and tells everyone upfront that the team will only move forward once every single person agrees on the choice.

Please select a leadership style for all five descriptions before submitting.
