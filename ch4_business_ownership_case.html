SECTION 1
Jordan's Food Truck — Sole Proprietorship
Jordan's situation: Jordan is 24 years old and wants to launch a food truck in downtown Fargo serving locally sourced tacos. Jordan plans to operate alone, keep costs low, and make all decisions independently. Jordan has $8,000 in personal savings to invest.
Question 1 of 15
Jordan decides to operate the food truck entirely on their own, with no partners or investors. Which form of business ownership best describes Jordan's business?
A General partnership
B Sole proprietorship
C S corporation
D Limited liability company
Question 2 of 15
After six months, the food truck is involved in an accident and a customer sues Jordan for $40,000. Jordan only has $8,000 in the business account. What is the most likely outcome for Jordan under a sole proprietorship?
A Jordan is protected because the business is a separate legal entity
B Jordan can only be sued up to the amount invested in the business
C Jordan's personal assets — savings, car, belongings — could be used to satisfy the judgment
D Jordan's liability is limited to $8,000 because that is all the business has
Question 3 of 15
Jordan's food truck earns $62,000 in profit during its first year. How will this income most likely be taxed under a sole proprietorship?
A It flows directly to Jordan's personal tax return and is taxed as personal income
B The business pays corporate income tax on the $62,000 before Jordan receives any
C The income is split between the business and Jordan, each paying taxes separately
D Sole proprietorships are exempt from federal income tax in the first two years
SECTION 2
Sam & Riley's Fitness Studio — Partnerships
Sam & Riley's situation: Best friends Sam and Riley decide to open "CoreFit Studio" in Moorhead. Sam has the fitness expertise and will run daily operations. Riley is a silent investor who contributes $25,000 but does not want to be involved in management decisions — and wants limited personal liability.
Question 4 of 15
Sam and Riley want a formal business structure. Given that Riley wants limited liability and no management role, which partnership structure fits them best?
A General partnership, with Sam and Riley as co-equal general partners
B Limited partnership, with Sam as the general partner and Riley as a limited partner
C Sole proprietorship under Sam's name, with Riley as an informal investor
D C corporation, because only corporations can offer limited liability
Question 5 of 15
Six months in, Sam and Riley have a major disagreement about pricing strategy. Sam wants to raise prices; Riley refuses to approve the change. Which is the most important reason attorneys recommend a written partnership agreement before starting a business?
A It is required by law before any partnership can open a bank account
B It protects against competition from other fitness studios in the area
C It guarantees the business will be profitable within the first year
D It establishes in advance how decisions are made, profits are split, and disputes are resolved — reducing conflict and legal risk
Question 6 of 15
CoreFit Studio earns $80,000 in profit. Sam and Riley agreed to split profits 60/40. Which statement about how partnership income is taxed is correct?
A The partnership pays corporate tax on the full $80,000, then distributes what remains
B Only the managing partner (Sam) pays taxes; silent investors are exempt
C Profits pass through to each partner's personal tax return — Sam pays tax on $48,000 and Riley on $32,000
D The partnership pays a flat 21% federal partnership tax before splitting profits
SECTION 3
Nova Tech — Corporations
Nova Tech's situation: Nova Tech is a software startup founded by three college friends. The company has grown fast, now employs 40 people, and needs to raise $2 million in capital. The founders want to protect their personal assets and are considering incorporating.
Question 7 of 15
Nova Tech's founders want to raise $2 million by selling ownership stakes to many investors. Which feature of a corporation makes this possible?
A Corporations can apply for unlimited government grants unavailable to other business types
B Corporations can issue stock, allowing them to raise large amounts of capital from many investors
C Corporations automatically receive bank loans at lower interest rates than other structures
D Corporations do not have to pay back investors if the business fails
Question 8 of 15
One of Nova Tech's early investors becomes angry when the product launch is delayed and threatens to sue the three founders personally for his $50,000 investment loss. Which corporate principle best protects the founders?
A Limited liability — a corporation is a separate legal entity, so the founders' personal assets are generally protected
B Double taxation — the corporation absorbs the loss and the founders owe nothing
C Perpetual existence — the corporation cannot be sued as long as it is still operating
D Proxy voting — shareholders can vote to dismiss the lawsuit on the founders' behalf
Question 9 of 15
Nova Tech earns $500,000 in profit. The company pays corporate income tax on that amount, then distributes $200,000 as dividends to shareholders — who then pay personal income tax on those dividends. What is this tax situation called?
A Pass-through taxation
B Progressive taxation
C Double taxation
D Corporate sheltering
Question 10 of 15
Nova Tech's board of directors votes to replace the CEO. One of the founders argues that as a 20% shareholder, they should make this decision alone. Who actually has the legal authority to hire and fire the CEO?
A The largest individual shareholder has final authority over all major decisions
B The board of directors — elected by shareholders — is responsible for major decisions like hiring and firing the CEO
C The state government that issued the corporate charter must approve all executive changes
D All employees vote equally on executive leadership decisions in a corporation
SECTION 4
LLCs & Franchises
Two new developments: Jordan (the food truck owner) is considering converting to an LLC for better liability protection while keeping a simple tax setup. Meanwhile, a national fitness brand called IronCore approaches Sam with a franchise offer to expand CoreFit into three more cities.
Question 11 of 15
Jordan wants liability protection like a corporation but does not want the complexity of a full corporation or double taxation. Which business structure is the best fit?
A C corporation — it offers the strongest liability protection available
B General partnership — adding a partner removes Jordan's personal liability
C LLC (Limited Liability Company) — it combines liability protection with pass-through taxation and less administrative burden
D S corporation — it is always the simplest and cheapest structure for a single owner
Question 12 of 15
IronCore offers Sam the right to open three new fitness locations using the IronCore name, systems, and equipment — in exchange for an upfront fee and ongoing royalties. What type of business arrangement is this?
A A merger — IronCore and CoreFit would combine into one company
B A joint venture — two companies temporarily sharing a project
C An acquisition — IronCore would purchase Sam's business outright
D A franchise — Sam (franchisee) pays IronCore (franchisor) for the right to operate under their brand and system
SECTION 5
Nova Tech — Mergers, Acquisitions & S-Corps
Nova Tech's next chapter: Nova Tech has grown to 200 employees and is now considering major structural moves — including a potential merger with a competitor, an acquisition offer from a large tech firm, and advice from their accountant about switching to S corporation status.
Question 13 of 15
Nova Tech's biggest competitor, DataStream, proposes combining both companies into one new, larger organization where both sets of owners share control. What type of corporate restructuring is this?
A Acquisition — Nova Tech is purchasing DataStream outright
B Merger — two companies combine to form one entity, with shared ownership
C Divestiture — Nova Tech is selling off part of its operations
D Leveraged buyout — employees are purchasing Nova Tech using borrowed funds
Question 14 of 15
A large technology firm offers to purchase Nova Tech completely — paying the founders and shareholders a set price per share so the firm gains full ownership and control. What is this called?
A Merger
B Joint venture
C Acquisition
D Franchise agreement
Question 15 of 15
Nova Tech's accountant recommends converting to an S corporation to avoid double taxation. Which of the following is a key requirement that limits whether a corporation can elect S corporation status?
A S corporations are limited to 100 shareholders and shareholders must be U.S. citizens or residents
B S corporations must be publicly traded on a stock exchange to qualify
C S corporations must have at least 500 shareholders to be approved by the IRS
D S corporation status is only available to non-profit organizations
