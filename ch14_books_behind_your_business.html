The owner's mini business plan decisions

These are the same decisions you are making in your own plan. You'll see each of them show up in the books.

Part 1: Who does what in accounting

Accounting is how a business collects, records, classifies, summarizes, reports, and analyzes its financial activity. Choose the term that best matches each situation at your business.

Part 2: Sort the accounts

The balance sheet is built on the accounting equation: Assets = Liabilities + Owners' equity. Classify each item from the business's records.

Current asset: cash, or something that will become cash or be used up within a year
Fixed asset: long-term, physical items used to run the business
Intangible asset: long-term items with no physical form
Current liability: a debt due within a year
Long-term liability: a debt due in more than a year
Owners' equity: the owner's investment plus profits kept in the business

Part 3: Build the income statement

The income statement shows revenues, expenses, and profit or loss over a period of time. Use the October records below.

Net sales = Gross sales − discounts  ·  Gross profit = Net sales − Cost of goods sold  ·  Net profit = Gross profit − Total operating expenses

Part 4: Build the balance sheet

The balance sheet is a snapshot of what the business owns and owes on one date. These items are in no particular order. Sort them yourself, then total them.

Part 5: Follow the cash

The statement of cash flows sorts cash coming in and going out into three activities.

Operating: day-to-day buying and selling
Investing: buying or selling long-term assets
Financing: borrowing, repaying loans, and owner money going in or out

Part 6: Read the ratios

Ratio analysis turns your statements into numbers a lender or investor can compare. Use your answers from Parts 3 and 4.

Current ratio (liquidity) = Current assets ÷ Current liabilities
Net profit margin (profitability) = Net profit ÷ Net sales × 100
Debt-to-equity (debt) = Total liabilities ÷ Owners' equity
