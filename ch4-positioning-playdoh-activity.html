1 The Play-Doh Story

Before it was a toy, Play-Doh was a wallpaper cleaner. In the 1930s, Kutol Products sold a soft, pliable putty that scrubbed coal soot off wallpaper. By the 1950s, coal heating (and soot-covered wallpaper) was disappearing, and the product was headed for the discontinued bin. A nursery school teacher discovered that kids loved molding the leftover cleaning putty, and Kutol repositioned the entire product around that insight: same core formula, completely different frame of reference, completely different customer.

Old positioning: A household cleaning product for adults who heat with coal.
New positioning: A safe, reusable creative-play compound for young children and the parents/teachers who supply them.
What didn't change: The physical product itself — the formula stayed nearly identical.
What did change: The target market, the frame of reference, and the points of difference the brand chose to emphasize.

This is the same repositioning logic we discussed in lecture with other brands — a company doesn't always need a new product to find a new position, sometimes it needs a new story about the same product.
